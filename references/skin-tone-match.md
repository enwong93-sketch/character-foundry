# Match skin tone across the neck seam

Use this procedure after the head fits, or when the head or body material changes. Choose the approved color source. The body is the usual source for matching the head and neck. If the head has the approved tone, use it as the source and adjust only exposed skin on the body.

1. **Identify actual skin visually.** Inspect the current model from enough angles to recognize the surface and its borders. Choose a clean patch on genuine exposed skin, such as an uncovered upper arm or cheek. Exclude clothing, gloves, jewelry, makeup, colored markings, highlights, strong shadows, and contact edges. If no patch can be identified, record UNVERIFIED and use an approved reference if available.
2. **Sample the base color.** Map several points inside that patch to the correct faces and UVs, then read the base-color texture and any material factor. Do the same on neutral skin in the target. Work in one color space and use robust representative samples. Screenshot RGB includes lighting, so use screenshots to identify and review skin rather than to pick its base color. Record the faces or UVs, texture revision and representative values.
3. **Adjust only target skin.** Use a reversible material adjustment on the working asset. For clean, comparable sampled patches, use the approved linear RGB median divided by the target linear RGB median per channel as an initial multiplier. Apply it only inside the skin region and confirm the result visually. Near-zero target channels, clipping, or heavily different baked shading require a different sample or a controlled color adjustment. Preserve tone variation, texture detail, transparency, UVs, geometry, and Shape Keys. If skin shares an atlas with clothing, eyes, lashes, brows, or accessories, restrict the adjustment to confirmed skin faces or a verified UV mask. Keep the original textures. If a reliable mask is unavailable, record UNVERIFIED before any adjustment to the shared material.
4. **Compare under the same lighting and view settings.** Inspect front, sides and rear, with close views where the neck meets the collar. Check that the transition looks natural and protected materials still match their originals. A material preview and an exported asset have separate acceptance results. If an export is requested, reopen it and inspect the color there too.

Record the source and target, where the source skin was identified, sample color space, adjusted materials or mask, and matched-angle before/after views. Report geometric seam quality separately from skin color.
